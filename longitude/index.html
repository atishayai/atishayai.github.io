Cross-generational · Thread T5

The prayer, twice

His great-grandmother, born 1930, wrote the Navkar Mantra in her journal as a girl to feel protected during British patrols. Seventy years later, he filled spiral notebooks with the same prayer to quiet his mind. Neither knew. The two documents sit nine years apart in the archive — only a full-corpus pass puts them side by side.

The money lexicon runs strongest in the earliest eras and recedes across the decade — until the late corpus critiques commodification instead of enacting it.

Resolution · Technology thread

The 2019 contradiction that wasn’t

At thirteen, four months apart: a research paper arguing AI restores connection for isolated elderly patients, and an essay titled “Technology Contributes to a Quarantined Life.” Read together, both defend the same value — human connection — and disagree only about the mechanism. Carried as a contradiction; resolved by the corpus.

Method. Layer one: qualitative thematic coding (5 threads, 6 behavioral codes) applied in a 100% close-reading pass. Layer two: 21 word-stem lexicons across five families — emotional tone, character, voice, intellect, identity — measured per 1,000 words, word-weighted by era. Lexicon hits are surface signals, not meaning-judgments; the dashboard pairs every trend with the highest-density source sentences so the measurement can be checked against the text. Rates shown are from the era-weighted computational pass; arcs described without figures are direction-of-trend from the same pass, corroborated by the coding layer. Corpus hygiene: 15+ saved-but-not-authored documents, 30+ revision duplicates, and one misattributed byline were identified and excluded from authorial analysis.
